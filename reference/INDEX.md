# Burnout Paradise Visual Reference

Private research reference for visual comparison against a Three.js clone.
Sources: EA/Criterion press stills served from the Steam store CDN (`shared.akamai.steamstatic.com`, apps `24740` Burnout Paradise: The Ultimate Box and `1238080` Burnout Paradise Remastered) and player captures from the Steam Community screenshot galleries for those two apps.
Audio in `audio/` is CC0 from the Soundscape/SSE Library collections on archive.org, not from the game itself - it is a tonal target, not a rip.

All images verified as real JPEG, >= 1280 px wide, >= 150 KB.

Tarmac is lit almost entirely by skylight, not sun, so it reads cool blue-grey while the car's roof and rear deck catch a warm rim from behind; the lane paint is worn and broken, never a clean white stripe. Guardrails and utility poles get long thin shadows that fall across three lanes and stay parallel - that parallelism is what sells a directional sun. Road surface has visible per-lane wear polish (darker, glossier tracks where tires run) separated by lighter, rougher aggregate between lanes. |

Road grain is legible even in the far third of frame - the aggregate does not dissolve into flat grey, which means the surface texture holds mip detail into the distance. |
| `dusk-highway-chase-04.jpg` | dusk-highway-chase | Low chase/pursuit cam behind an orange sport coupe on an overpass, downtown skyline ahead, dusk | Camera sits low (~0.8 m) so the road fills the bottom third with strong perspective foreshortening, and the radial blur origin is offset toward the car, not the frame center. Tower glass ahead is a two-tone reflection - warm sky on upper floors, cool ground bounce below - rather than a single flat env-map colour. Guard-rail and overpass concrete are dirt-mapped with vertical drip streaks under every joint, which stops the geometry from reading as clean primitives. |
| `boost-blur-01.jpg` | boost-blur | Boost active, car airborne off a ramp against clear sky, twin exhaust jets firing | Boost flame is a two-stage emitter: a short blue-white core at the tip and a longer green-yellow plume that trails and smears into a soft streak from the car's own motion. The flame casts no scene light (it is additive-only) but does bloom against the sky, which is the exact cheat to copy. Orange metallic body shows a clear metallic-flake response - broad soft highlight from the sky dome plus a tight specular streak along the shoulder line - and the underbody is caked in dust while the upper panels stay clean. |
| `boost-blur-02.jpg` | boost-blur | Chase cam behind a red supercar under heavy boost, industrial waterfront, daylight | Radial/zoom blur is strong but masked off the hero car, and the mask edge is soft, so the car appears to punch a hole through the blur field rather than sitting on top of it. Blur kernel length scales with radial distance from the vanishing point - near the road edge it is 40+ px, at center it is nearly zero. Chromatic fringing appears only in the outer ~20% of frame, red-cyan on high-contrast edges like crane booms and lane markings. |
| `boost-blur-03.jpg` | boost-blur | Boost run through a concrete tunnel, oncoming traffic, extreme speed streaks | Tunnel ceiling strip lights smear into continuous white bands that converge on the vanishing point - the streaks are geometry-aligned, not a screen-space radial fake, so they curve with the tunnel. Everything except the hero car is doubled and ghosted by multi-tap accumulation blur rather than a single directional smear. Value range collapses toward the vanishing point (heavy bloom + veiling glare) so the far end of the tunnel is a blown white disc, which reads as eye adaptation lagging behind speed. |
| `boost-blur-04.jpg` | boost-blur | Chase cam on a cliffside pass, boost on, twin red-orange exhaust flames, HUD present | Exhaust flames are short, wide, and orange-red here (a different boost tier than `-01`), pulsing with visible per-frame length variation, and they light nothing but themselves. Rock face on the right is high-frequency normal-mapped with self-shadowing crevices while the road ahead has a distinct wet-dark centre from the shaded canyon - two very different roughness values in one shot. The yellow centre line is chipped and discontinuous, and its specular breaks up rather than staying uniformly matte. |
| `crash-cam-01.jpg` | crash-cam | Takedown cam, slow-mo, rival car launched airborne through a fence while the hero car drives under | Deformation is real mesh damage, not a swapped decal: the rival's roof is creased, its door hangs open on a bent hinge, and the panel gaps have widened asymmetrically. Debris is a mix of large tumbling shards with individual motion blur and a fine particle dust cloud that catches the low sun and reads as translucent, not as flat grey sprites. Slow-mo drops the shutter angle, so the airborne car is sharp while the road under it still streaks - the mismatch is the signature of a time-dilated crash cam. |
| `crash-cam-02.jpg` | crash-cam | Post-crash cam, wrecked blue car settled against a chevron barrier, "WRECKED" banner, dusk | The wreck is bent along a single primary impact axis with secondary crumple in the front-left quarter; the wheel has torn off and lies separately with its own contact shadow. A grey smoke column rises with soft self-shadowing and thins with height, and it is lit by the warm sunset rather than being uniform grey. The tarmac around the impact carries darkened scuff and skid deposit that is baked into the surface, not a decal floating above it. |
| `crash-cam-03.jpg` | crash-cam | Extreme close crash cam, slow-mo, white car crushed head-on into a green car, cracked glass | Panel crumple shows real thin-sheet-metal behaviour: long shallow buckles with sharp fold ridges catching a hard specular line, and the paint stays glossy across the deformed surface instead of going matte. Windshield fractures into a dense radial spiderweb with a bright caustic sparkle on each crack line. Depth of field is aggressive - only the impact zone is in focus, the far end of the car falls off into a smooth bokeh, which is what makes the camera read as a physical lens rather than a game view. |
| `crash-cam-04.jpg` | crash-cam | Crash cam, two red cars locked head-on, debris fanning out, overcast/dusk | Both cars share the same red family but read as different materials - one clean clearcoat, one dulled and dirt-streaked - so the wreck does not look like one duplicated asset. Torn bodywork exposes an interior dark cavity with no light leak, and the bumper has separated and rotated rather than simply intersecting the other mesh. Golden alloy wheels are the brightest specular in frame and the only element with a sharp mirror-like highlight, which anchors the exposure. |
| `wet-night-asphalt-01.jpg` | wet-night-asphalt | Night, car parked under a neon "Billiards" sign, wet forecourt, headlights on | Neon tubes are the light sources and they produce coloured vertical smears on the wet ground directly below each sign, with the smear length proportional to sign height - true reflections, not a flat tinted decal. Pink and cyan neon spills onto the car's flank as a wide, low-intensity coloured wash that survives all the way into the shadow side, so the car is never black. Headlights throw two hard hot cones with a visible bright ellipse where they hit the pavement plus a fainter volumetric cone in the air. Ground is a mix of dry matte patches and glossy wet patches, so roughness varies spatially instead of one uniform wet shader. |
| `wet-night-asphalt-02.jpg` | wet-night-asphalt | Night/blue-hour highway curve, red coupe, streetlight pools on damp tarmac, 5000 px ultrawide | Each sodium streetlight lays down an elongated elliptical pool that stretches away from the camera and overlaps the next one, and the overlap region is visibly brighter - additive light accumulation on the road, not per-light clamping. The damp surface has an anisotropic sheen streaked along the direction of travel, so reflections elongate down-road rather than mirroring symmetrically. Sky retains a thin band of residual sunset at the horizon while the zenith is fully night, and the road's ambient tint is pulled from that sky, keeping the shadows blue rather than neutral black. |
| `wet-night-asphalt-03.jpg` | wet-night-asphalt | Night multi-lane freeway, online race, long reflective light streaks on the road, HUD present | Tail lights and roadside lamps become long thin vertical specular streaks on the road, and the streaks stay locked to their source as the camera moves - the hallmark of a real screen-space or planar reflection rather than a bloom sprite. Reflection intensity falls off with viewing angle: near the camera the road is nearly matte, at the horizon it goes almost mirror. Boost bars, name tags, and HUD text sit at full saturation over a dim scene without picking up any of the scene's bloom, which is the correct HUD compositing pass. |
| `wet-night-asphalt-04.jpg` | wet-night-asphalt | Night, pink convertible in front of a neon-bulb-lit warehouse frontage, Motor City | Individual incandescent bulbs along the building read as small bright points with a tight bloom kernel and a subtle warm halo, and they do not merge into one blown strip - the bloom threshold is high and the radius small. Yellow signage is emissive but still shows its underlying diffuse texture, so it looks like a lit surface rather than a self-illuminated flat colour. The car's pink paint is lit almost entirely by that warm signage on one side and by cold blue night sky ambient on the other, giving a clean warm/cool split across the body - the single strongest cue for night lighting. |
| `daytime-downtown-01.jpg` | daytime-downtown | High noon, downtown intersection, palms, crosswalk, traffic, buildings on both sides | Sun is near-overhead so shadows are short, hard, and directly under objects, and every prop (traffic light, palm, parked car) has a correctly grounded contact shadow with a darker ambient-occlusion core. Building facades show flat unbloomed albedo while only the glass and chrome trim reach highlight intensity, so the frame has a wide but controlled value range. Road has a genuine aggregate texture with tar-seam lines, patched repairs, and a black rubber-scuffed apron at the intersection - it never reads as flat grey. Prop density is high (signage, wires, hydrants, planters) and the wires cast their own thin shadows. |
| `daytime-downtown-02.jpg` | daytime-downtown | Downtown street canyon looking down a boulevard, skyscrapers, dense billboards, low warm sun | The street canyon is in full shade while the upper third of the towers is in direct sun, producing a hard horizontal light/shade boundary partway up the buildings - vertical light gradient is the thing that makes a city block feel tall. Billboards and neon are lit but not yet emissive-dominant (it is still day), so they read as painted surfaces with slight glow rather than light sources. Glass towers reflect the sky and neighbouring buildings with a slight blue-green cast and visible per-panel variation, so the curtain wall is not one mirror plane. |
| `daytime-downtown-03.jpg` | daytime-downtown | Downtown block, open-wheel race car on a wide painted-yellow intersection, bright day | Huge painted yellow box-junction markings on the road are worn, scuffed, and partly abraded away, and their specular differs from the surrounding tarmac - paint is glossier and slightly raised. Storefront signage and screens run the full colour gamut against neutral concrete, which is what keeps the frame from going monochrome. Exposed-suspension geometry on the race car casts a dense stack of small overlapping shadows onto the road, a good stress test for shadow-map resolution. |
| `daytime-downtown-04.jpg` | daytime-downtown | Low three-quarter view of a red muscle car on a downtown street, HAZY DUSK / BLUE HOUR (**not** a bright day - label corrected wave R, `verdicts/wave-r/resolver.md` §2), buildings receding | **TIME-OF-DAY WARNING, read before you anchor anything on this frame.** The sun is at or below the horizon and the illuminant is the horizon glow, not a sun disc. Zenith strip `0.05,0.95,0.00,0.05` reads rgb **0.5,36.3,59.4**, luma p50 **34.4**, with **99.97%** of pixels under luma 40 and the R channel at 0.5/255 (B/R = 119); `daytime-downtown-01`, high noon, reads **53.3,133.7,180.1** p50 **122.2** at the identical region. Clean-sky patch `0.30,0.75,0.01,0.09` is **1.6,44.8,72.1** p50 36.7 against `-02`'s 62,118.8,142.6 p50 108.4, i.e. 3.0x darker. The near-horizon sky `0.42,0.52,0.44,0.49` is **229,218.8,214.7** with 100% of pixels >= 200, so zenith-to-horizon luma runs 36.7 -> 221.6, a **6.0x** gradient against `-02`'s 2.1x. No directional sun: lit road p50 166.2 against the car's own cast shadow p50 134.6, a ratio of **0.81** (19% darkening) with a shapeless soft penumbra. Street lamps, headlights and the tower's "R R" sign are all UNLIT, so this is late civil twilight before the lights come on. This frame anchors **no** daytime luma, chroma, sobel, `dark%` or row-fraction target; `-02` is the camera-situation match and `-01` the lighting-class match. The haze half of the old label is correct, and the observations below are still good AT ITS OWN DUSK GRADE: aerial perspective is doing real work: near buildings are saturated and contrasty, far buildings desaturate toward the sky colour and lose contrast, with the falloff smooth and exponential rather than a hard fog plane. Exposed engine chrome and the car's red clearcoat are the only two high-gloss materials in frame and both pick up a distinctly different environment reflection. The road surface directly under the car darkens into a soft occlusion gradient rather than a hard drop shadow, so the car sits in the scene instead of on it. |
| `car-paint-closeup-01.jpg` | car-paint-closeup | Tight three-quarter front on a red/yellow liveried race car, under a bridge, headlight lit | Clearcoat is doing two separate things at once: a broad soft diffuse-plus-flake response across the panels, and a razor-thin white specular line running the full length of the shoulder crease. Livery decals sit *under* the clearcoat - the specular streak crosses the decal boundary without breaking, which is the detail most clones get wrong. Headlight lens shows an internal reflector pattern plus a warm bloom, and there is a subtle red bounce from the body panel onto the ground under the front wing. |
| `car-paint-closeup-02.jpg` | car-paint-closeup | Three-quarter front, white car with red graphic livery, bright daylight, palms behind | White paint stays white without clipping - the panels sit around 85-92% luminance with the highlight rolling off smoothly, which requires real tonemapping rather than a clamp. Fresnel is clearly visible: panels facing the camera are near-flat white while panels raking away pick up a strong sky reflection and go blue-tinted at the grazing edge. Wheel faces are polished metal with a sharp anisotropic ring highlight, and the tyre sidewall is genuinely rough - near-zero specular right next to the wheel's mirror finish. |
| `car-paint-closeup-03.jpg` | car-paint-closeup | Pure side profile of a silver sedan with dark graphics, dusk industrial backdrop, motion blur | Silver metallic shows the classic bright-band-plus-dark-band split: sky reflection along the upper flank, dark ground reflection along the rocker, with a fast transition at the body's horizon line. Graphics are gloss-matched to the base coat, so the specular sweep runs unbroken across both. Background is heavily blurred while the car is sharp, and the blur is a lateral streak - a panning shot, meaning the blur must be velocity-based per object rather than a full-screen effect. |
| `car-paint-closeup-04.jpg` | car-paint-closeup | Three-quarter front of a blue/red graphic-wrapped coupe on a suburban street, 2560x1600 | Two-tone wrap with a hard colour boundary that the specular highlight crosses without interruption - proves paint gloss is independent of base colour. Chrome-faced wheels function as mirrors and pick up the palm trees and the sky, giving a legible curved reflection of the environment rather than a static cubemap smear. Windscreen shows a layered result: sky reflection on the outer surface, a dim visible interior underneath, plus a subtle grime/streak layer, which is what stops glass from reading as a flat blue plane. |
| `hud-overlay-01.jpg` | hud-overlay | Chase cam, stationary at a junction, full HUD: dual event timers, boost bar, minimap, district banner | The full Burnout HUD vocabulary in one frame - green segmented boost bar bottom-left, rotated minimap card bottom-right with the district name plate above it, event timers top-right, street-name plate above the horizon. HUD elements are drawn at native resolution with crisp 1 px edges over a bloomed, motion-blurred scene, so they are unmistakably a separate post-blur pass. Boost bar is not a plain rectangle: it has a jagged hand-drawn outline, an internal segment texture, and a slight glow that reads as part of the game's paper/marker art direction. |
| `hud-overlay-02.jpg` | hud-overlay | Online race, chase cam on a downtown freeway, full HUD with position, rival list, boost bar, minimap | Race-state HUD at maximum density: position indicator top-left, live rival leaderboard top-right with per-player distance, boost bar bottom-left, minimap bottom-right, plus world-space floating name tags over other cars. The name tags are the interesting case - they are billboarded in world space and scale with distance, yet render at HUD saturation and are never occluded by geometry. All HUD panels share one skewed, slightly-rotated card layout with soft drop shadows rather than a flat rectangular overlay. |
| `hud-overlay-03.jpg` | hud-overlay | Chase cam on an arterial road toward downtown, objective HUD: challenge target, timer, minimap, boost bar | Cleanest read of the boost bar in the set - green fill, segmented notches, jagged brush-stroke border, sitting directly on top of a heavily motion-blurred road with zero blur applied to itself. Left-side objective stack (goal, elapsed time, running counters) is left-aligned monospace-feeling text with a subtle dark scrim behind it so it stays legible over both bright sky and dark tarmac. Minimap is a rotated 3D-perspective card, not a flat top-down circle - it tilts with the road ahead. |
| `hud-overlay-04.jpg` | hud-overlay | Stunt run, car airborne and spinning off a cliff road, HUD showing score, target, timer, multiplier, minimap | Stunt-mode HUD: target/current score and timer stacked left, a large animated score-popup token ("4450") centre-right drawn over a torn-paper splatter graphic, boost bar bottom-left, minimap bottom-right. The score popup is a screen-space element that still tracks the car's approximate position, and it has its own scale-and-fade animation independent of the scene. Dust plume behind the car is lit and semi-transparent, and the HUD renders cleanly on top of it - a good test that transparency sorting and the HUD pass are properly separated. |
